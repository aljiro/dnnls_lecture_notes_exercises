## Reveal: what was this experiment really about?

Week 4 stopped before image retrieval because students did not yet have a principled way to turn an image into learned features. Now they do.

The frozen convolutional encoder lets us compare frames in a learned visual representation space. This makes retrieval a legitimate first use of the encoder: no decoder is required and no future-frame generator has been smuggled into the task.

But the experiment also exposes an important limit. **Representation is not reasoning.** A visual encoder can tell us which frames look alike. It does not, by itself, know how four observations combine over time, which character is likely to reappear, or what camera shot should logically come next. This is why near-copy windows are naturally easier for visual matching than shot cuts.

The system now has a visual currency, but not yet a mechanism for combining information across a sequence.
