## Reveal: what was this experiment really about?

An autoregressive language model represents a distribution over the **next token given previous tokens**. Generation requires an additional decision about how to choose from that distribution.

Greedy decoding repeatedly takes the single highest-probability token. On a repetitive corpus this can drive many generations toward the same modal phrases. Sampling does not make the underlying model smarter; it reveals more of the distribution the model already learned.

This distinction matters later when conditional models appear to produce the same sentence for many inputs: the problem could lie in the model, in its use of the condition, or simply in how we decode it.
