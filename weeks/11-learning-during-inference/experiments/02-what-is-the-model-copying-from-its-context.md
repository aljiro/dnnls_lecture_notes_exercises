## Reveal: what was this experiment really about?

A useful way to understand some forms of in-context behaviour is as **retrieval and copying inside the context**. The query contains a pattern; attention can route information from an earlier matching pattern toward the current prediction.

The intervention mattered more than the picture. Changing the label attached to the matching key asks a causal behavioural question: does the output follow the information we think was retrieved? Conflicting and distance-controlled contexts then reveal that content matching can coexist with positional or recency biases.

This is **induction-style behaviour**, not proof that we found a canonical induction circuit or an explanation of all in-context learning. The broader lesson is methodological: mechanistic hypotheses should survive interventions, not merely attractive visualisations.
