## Reveal: what was this experiment really about?

A deterministic autoencoder learns a **representation and reconstruction mapping**, but it does not automatically tell us how to draw new latent codes.

The VAE adds a probabilistic structure: each input defines an approximate posterior and the KL term encourages those posteriors to remain compatible with a simple prior. That constraint can slightly hurt reconstruction while making prior sampling meaningful.

Now ask Gemini:

> Using our reconstruction losses, latent statistics and random-sample grids, explain why the ordinary autoencoder was useful for representation learning but did not define a clean generative sampling procedure. Then explain how the VAE changes the training objective so that sampling from a prior becomes meaningful.

### Final discussion questions

1. Why is a good decoder not sufficient for generation?
2. What is sacrificed when the latent space is regularised toward a prior?
3. What would happen if the KL term became effectively zero throughout training?
4. How does this connect to the idea that a generative model should define a distribution, not just a compression code?
